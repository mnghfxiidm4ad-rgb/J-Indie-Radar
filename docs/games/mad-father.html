What it is

Mad Father is Sen's horror adventure starring Aya, a girl in a mansion that is also a laboratory, with a father whose love and whose crimes occupy the same operating table. You sneak, solve, read, and decide how much of the family you can stand to save. It is one of the Japanese RPG Maker titles that broke out of freeware circles into a durable Steam afterlife, alongside Misao and the broader Sen-adjacent horror wave. The tone is gothic rather than joke-trap: corpses, experiments, a town that should have noticed. Japanese players recommend it when someone asks for horror with a plot they will remember, not only a jumpscare compilation. Overseas players often arrive from Ib and find Mad Father heavier, wordier, and more interested in guilt as a mechanic.

Deep-dive systems analysis

Aya's toolkit is adventure-game: items, hiding, a few confrontations that are really puzzle bosses. The systems that matter are visibility, when a threat is in the corridor, and ending flags: who is alive, what you witnessed, whether you treated the father as monster, victim, or both. Chase sequences are the execution tax. They are not a soulslike, but they will kill a first-time player who does not learn room geometry. Diary pages are not flavor. They are map keys and moral context. Sen's design assumes you will read. Japanese character discussion around Aya and the father is the long tail of the game's popularity; the Steam page is a portal into that argument. Compared with Corpse Party, the cast is smaller and the space is a single cursed home rather than a school-dimension. Compared with Witch's House, deaths are less slapstick and more narrative. The English release's job was to carry that argument across language. It mostly does, which is why the title still sells on story-horror lists.

Why it is trending in Japan

  • Family-horror as a complete package. Japanese reviews still cite the father-daughter theme as stronger than the average RPG Maker shock. It is a this-one-has-writing recommendation.
  • Sen's brand is a horror cluster. Players who finish Mad Father get sent to Misao and related titles. The cluster effect keeps each Steam page feeding the others in Japanese what-next threads.
  • Chase scenes are clip-friendly. Tension-and-release corridors perform well on video. That discoverability is unglamorous and effective, especially when a new horror fan asks for Japanese classics.

Language barrier

Medium (Screen Translation OK)

English localization is available on Steam. Story is the product: diaries, family talk, moral choices. Japanese is the original voice; English is how most overseas players should play. Without either, High: you will fail flags and miss why anyone cares. Puzzles are still object-based, chases are visual. Screen translation of a Japanese UI can limp to an ending but will butcher the father-daughter thesis. Use the official English.

Playtime and difficulty

Five to nine hours. Medium difficulty: chases plus reading. Multiple endings add replays. Not a 40-hour RPG. Emotional difficulty can exceed mechanical difficulty.

Who this is for

Story-horror players, RPG Maker canon hunters, people who want Japanese indie horror with a family-sized tragedy. Content notes: medical gore, child peril, parental abuse themes in fiction. Skip if you only wanted cute ghosts.
